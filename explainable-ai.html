Explainable AI

Explainable AI (XAI) is an area of artificial intelligence focused on making the decisions and predictions of AI systems easier for people to understand and interpret

What XAI is

Explainable AI (XAI) – Methods that help people understand the outputs or behaviour of an AI or machine-learning system.

  • Can be particularly useful when a model produces a result but its internal decision-making process is difficult for a person to understand.
Black-Box Model – A model whose internal processes are difficult to interpret directly.
  • An explanation method can provide information about how the model behaved for a particular prediction.
Interpretability – How understandable a model or its behaviour is to a person.
  • Different models and explanation methods can provide different levels and types of understanding.
  • For SAGEXP, explainability is important because a user may want to understand more than simply being told that a stress-related prediction has been made.

Post-hoc explanations

Post-hoc Explanation – Produced after a machine-learning model has already been trained.

  • Instead of changing the original model, the explanation method examines its inputs and outputs to provide an understandable description of a prediction.
These are useful when the original model is already built and cannot easily be understood by looking directly at its internal structure.
  • For SAGEXP, a model receives several physiological measurements, it produces a stress-related classification.
Post-hoc explanations can make a complex model's output easier to discuss without requiring the user to understand the model's full mathematical structure.
  • However, a post-hoc explanation should not automatically be treated as a perfect description of the model's internal reasoning.
  • Some explanation methods approximate model behaviour and have limitations.

Types of Post-Hoc explanations

LIME (Local Interpretable Model-Agnostic Explanations)

  • Focuses on one prediction.
  • Creates small variations around the input being examined, observes how the original model's prediction changes, and uses a simpler model to approximate the original model's behaviour in that area.
SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations)
  • Assigns contribution values to features for a prediction.
  • These values are intended to show how individual features of an input contributed to the model's output.

Local vs Global explanations

Local Explanation – Focuses on one specific prediction and aims to explain why the model produced that particular result for that particular input.

  • A local explanation is similar to asking, “Why did the model make this decision this time?”
Global Explanation – Looks at the model's behaviour more broadly and aims to describe general patterns in how the model makes predictions.
  • A global explanation is more like asking, “How does this model generally make decisions?”
For SAGEXP, local explanations are particularly relevant because the user may want to understand a specific stress classification produced from their current measurements.

The limits of AI explanations

AI explanations have many limitations, post-hoc explanations can be approximations of model behaviour rather than a direct view of everything happening inside the model.
Therefore:

  • An explanation does not guarantee that the model's prediction is correct.
    • A model can make an incorrect prediction and still produce an explanation for it.
  • Different explanation methods can produce different forms of information, and no single method is guaranteed to provide a complete understanding of every model.
  • A local explanation only describes a particular prediction.
    • It should not automatically be treated as a complete description of how the model behaves in every situation.

    • There is also a difference between giving an explanation and giving a useful explanation.
    • For a general user, an explanation needs to communicate relevant information clearly without requiring technical knowledge.
    • For SAGEXP, this means an explanation should help the user understand what the classification means and what information contributes to the conclusions made.
